Method

How the score is built, and what it cannot tell you.

The score is arithmetic over columns an attorney filled in from primary sources. Nothing is inferred by a model. Every component is shown on the measure's own brief so the derivation can be checked against the statute.

Axis 1 — claim exposure (0–100)

Six components, weighted by how strongly each predicts that a statute produces a claim tendered to an insurer.

Axis 2 — insurability friction

Not scored, flagged. These features do not make a claim more likely; they make the claim harder to cover once it arrives.

  • Criminal exposure — engages the criminal-acts and intentional-acts exclusions; often reduces the question to defence costs.
  • Punitive damages — uninsurable by statute or public policy in a number of states; the most-favoured-venue wording decides.
  • Disgorgement or restitution — frequently held not to be "damages" or "loss" at all.
  • Civil penalties and fines — insurable-where-permitted wording and sublimits govern; many towers exclude them outright.
  • Waiver or indemnity restricted — the statute limits contracting out, so the risk cannot be pushed down the vendor chain.
  • Assessment discoverability — an impact assessment becomes a claim document; bears on defensibility and on reserving.

Axis 3 — controls the insured can pull

The underwriting conversation. A statute with a cure period and a recognised-framework defence is a materially better risk than a bare private right of action, at the same exposure score.

  • Cure period — a window to fix before liability attaches.
  • Notice before suit — a pre-suit demand step that surfaces claims early.
  • Recognised framework defence — NIST AI RMF or ISO 42001 conformity pleaded as an affirmative defence. The strongest single control in this dataset, and rare.
  • Size or scale threshold — many statutes do not reach a small insured at all.
  • Record-retention duty — a duty, but also the evidence that defends the claim.
  • Stated limitations period — bounds the tail.

Weekly refresh

  1. The research pass produces INDEX.csv each Monday — one row per measure, 39 columns of primary-source findings.
  2. scripts/build_laws.py derives subject, regulated role, likely lines and the three axes, then diffs against the previous build.
  3. The diff is what gets reviewed: new rows and changed rows only. Unchanged rows are not re-read.
  4. The build writes the data into this page, so the page is one file with no backend and no runtime dependency.

What it cannot tell you

  • Silence is not safety. A statute silent on class treatment leaves the ordinary class rules in place. Illinois' BIPA says nothing about classes and is the largest source of AI-adjacent class exposure in the country.
  • The score has no view on frequency. It measures what the statute permits, not how often anyone has used it. A high-scoring statute nobody has sued on still scores high.
  • Line mapping is a checklist. It is derived from the subject of the statute, not from any policy. The wording, the retro date and the exclusions govern.
  • Dates are the researcher's reading. Where a cell records a staged commencement the earliest date is used. Rows flagged unconfirmed have not been checked against the enrolled text.
  • No federal layer. This index is state measures only. Federal preemption is recorded where a statute addresses it, and is otherwise an open question.
